The bigger picture

Behind every symptom search is a much larger animal health gap.

JeevRaksha is a small, symptom-matching tool — but it sits inside a much bigger story: millions of farm and companion animals across India that are hard to reach with timely veterinary care. This page lays out the animals we cover, the scale of the problem, what's already working, what's still missing, and the organisations doing the ground work.

The problem

India has one of the world's largest animal populations, but veterinary reach hasn't kept pace with it.

1 vet per ~5,000 animals

Rural veterinary care is stretched thin

The World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH/OIE) recommends roughly one veterinarian per 500 animals; rural India runs at a fraction of that, leaving many herds without regular preventive care.

₹23,000+ crore a year

Preventable outbreaks carry a heavy cost

ICAR has estimated that Foot-and-Mouth Disease alone costs the Indian economy over ₹23,000 crore annually — largely through lost milk yield, weight, and fertility, most of it preventable with earlier detection and vaccination.

~9.1 million bites / year

Rabies remains a live public-health issue

A national ICMR study recorded roughly 9.1 million animal bites and around 5,700 rabies deaths a year in India, disproportionately affecting children and rural households with limited access to post-exposure care.

Low

Disease reporting is inconsistent

Many animal diseases, including rabies in animals, aren't formally notifiable, so there's no organised national surveillance — outbreaks are often known locally long before they show up in any dataset.

What's already being done

There's a real ecosystem of government programmes, NGOs and grassroots efforts already working on this — JeevRaksha is meant to sit alongside them, not replace them.

National disease control programmes

Government schemes such as the National Animal Disease Control Programme run mass vaccination drives against FMD and brucellosis in cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats and pigs.

Animal Birth Control & rabies vaccination

Municipal and NGO-run ABC programmes sterilise and vaccinate stray dogs against rabies, the single biggest lever for reducing both the stray population and human rabies risk.

Mobile veterinary units and ambulances

Several NGOs and state programmes now run mobile clinics and ambulance services that bring basic diagnosis and treatment directly into villages and urban strays' territory.

Community awareness and symptom literacy

Farmer training, school outreach and simple tools like JeevRaksha aim to help people recognise early warning signs sooner, so animals reach a vet before a treatable illness becomes an outbreak.

What still needs fixing

The gaps are less about any single missing programme and more about reach, coordination and follow-through.

Last-mile veterinary access

Even where mobile units exist, remote and hilly regions are often visited too rarely to catch disease early — the vet shortage is a distance problem as much as a headcount problem.

Shared, real-time disease data

Government bodies, NGOs and private vets largely keep their own records; a common, real-time picture of where outbreaks are emerging would let resources move faster.

Affordable diagnostics

Confirming a suspected disease often still requires a lab far from where the animal is — faster, cheaper field diagnostics would shorten the gap between a symptom and a confirmed treatment plan.

Follow-through on post-exposure care

Studies show most bite victims get at least one rabies vaccine dose but many don't complete the full course or get immunoglobulin when needed — awareness needs to extend past "get vaccinated" to "finish the course."

NGOs working on this

A starting list of Indian animal welfare organisations doing rescue, veterinary care, sterilisation/vaccination and advocacy work. Search each name for your nearest chapter and current contact details.

Blue Cross of India

Rescue, Animal Birth Control, a full animal hospital and mobile ambulance services.

Delhi

Sanjay Gandhi Animal Care Centre

One of India's largest shelters, offering round-the-clock veterinary care for dogs, cats and birds.

Pan-India

People for Animals (PFA)

Andhra Pradesh

Karuna Society for Animals and Nature

Shelter and veterinary care, with vaccination and sterilisation drives in the region.

Jaipur, Rajasthan

Help in Suffering

Rescue and rehabilitation for abandoned and injured animals, alongside community education.

Pan-India

PETA India

Advocacy, policy work and public education on animal welfare and cruelty prevention.

National network

FIAPO — Federation of Indian Animal Protection Organisations

An umbrella body connecting animal welfare groups across the country on shared campaigns.

Wildlife SOS

Rescue and rehabilitation focused on wildlife, including former performing and captive animals.
